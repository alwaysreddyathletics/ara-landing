Section 02
The Athletic Rebuild Framework

Every decision in this program is built on four pillars. Understand them and the training makes sense. Skip them and you are just following a spreadsheet with no idea why.

PillarThe PrincipleWhat It Means in Practice
01 — Progressive Stress The body adapts to stress that is slightly beyond its current capacity. Too much breaks you. Too little does nothing. Every phase of this program increases demand across at least one variable — volume, intensity, or density. You will never train at the same level two phases in a row.
02 — Structured Recovery Fitness is not built during training. It is built during recovery. Training is the stimulus; sleep and food are the adaptation. Deload weeks are programmed, not optional. You will reduce volume by ~40% every fourth week. Most athletes want to skip this. Do not skip this.
03 — Specificity You get better at what you train. General fitness helps. Specific training is what produces specific results. As the program progresses, training becomes more specific to your goal. Early weeks build the base. Later weeks target the exact output you care about.
04 — Consistency Over Intensity The athlete who trains at 80% consistently for 12 weeks beats the athlete who trains at 100% for three weeks and breaks down. When life gets in the way, reduce intensity before you reduce frequency. Showing up at 70% is always better than not showing up. Use the RPE guide in each phase.

On RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)

Every session in this program is prescribed by effort level, not just pace or weight. RPE 6 means you could hold a conversation. RPE 8 means you could say a sentence but it would be uncomfortable. RPE 10 means maximal effort. Use these numbers honestly. Athletes who lie to themselves about RPE plateau fastest.

Section 04
Nutrition Protocol

This is not a diet. It is a fueling framework. The goal is to give your body enough of the right inputs to absorb the training, recover faster, and perform at a higher ceiling. Simple. Repeatable. Sustainable.

Daily Targets — Training Day
Protein
0.8–1g
per lb of bodyweight
The non-negotiable. Every meal should have a protein anchor. Chicken, eggs, Greek yogurt, fish, beef — pick your sources and hit the number.
Carbohydrates
2–3g
per lb of bodyweight
Your primary fuel. Prioritize around training: pre-run and post-session. Rice, oats, potatoes, fruit. Do not fear carbs during heavy training weeks.
Fats
0.4g
per lb of bodyweight
Support hormones and joint health. Olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish. Fill this target — do not chase zero fat as a performance strategy.
Timing Protocol
WindowWhat to EatWhy
90 min Pre-Training Carb-forward meal, moderate protein, low fat and fiber. Example: oats + banana + eggs. Fuel is in the bloodstream and muscles when the session starts. Avoid fat and fiber — they slow gastric emptying.
During (60+ min) 30–60g carbs per hour via gel, banana, or sports drink. Water throughout. Sessions over 60 minutes deplete glycogen. Replace it in real time or pay for it in the final third of the session.
Within 30 min Post 25–40g protein + fast carbs. Example: protein shake + fruit, or Greek yogurt + granola. The anabolic window is real for endurance athletes. Protein synthesis is elevated immediately post-session. Hit it.
Rest Day Adjustment Keep protein identical. Reduce carbs by 30–40%. Fat can increase slightly. You need fewer carbs when you are not burning them. Protein stays constant because recovery is still happening.
Hard Session Eve Carb-load the night before Tuesday intervals and Saturday long runs. Extra serving of rice, pasta, or sweet potato. Glycogen storage peaks at ~24 hours. Fill the tank the night before your hardest sessions.
The One Thing Most Athletes Get Wrong

Under-eating on heavy training weeks. If you are training 5 days a week and feeling flat, fatigued, or unable to hit RPE targets — eat more first, sleep more second. More volume is almost never the answer when the output is dropping. The Athletic Rebuild approach: always look at recovery inputs before you adjust training outputs.

Section 05
Recovery System

Recovery is not passive. It is part of the training stimulus. The athletes who recover well train harder with less breakdown. These are the non-negotiables.

Sleep

  • Minimum 7 hours. Target 8+.
  • Same sleep / wake time 7 days a week. Consistency matters more than duration.
  • Phone out of the bedroom. Non-negotiable for athletes who claim they "can't sleep."
  • If sleep is under 6 hours the night before a key session, reduce intensity by 1 RPE point.
  • Track it weekly in your check-in sheet. Low sleep predicts performance drops before you feel them.

Mobility & Soft Tissue

  • 10 min mobility routine daily — hips, ankles, thoracic spine. Do it in the morning before coffee if you need a trigger.
  • Foam roll post-session: calves, quads, IT band, glutes. 60 seconds per area.
  • Dynamic warm-up before every run — leg swings, hip circles, A-skip, B-skip. Do not skip this in the name of time.
  • Static stretching post-session only. Never pre-session cold.
  • If a muscle feels tight for 3+ consecutive days, reduce training on that day and address it.

Stress Management

  • Training stress and life stress are the same to your nervous system. When life is hard, back the training off before you break down.
  • Rate your readiness score each morning (1–10) in your check-in sheet. Below 5 = easy day, no exceptions.
  • Breathing protocol: 5 minutes of box breathing (4 in, 4 hold, 4 out, 4 hold) post hard sessions to bring HRV down.
  • Cold exposure (cold shower, 2–3 min) shown to reduce perceived fatigue between sessions.
  • Alcohol impairs recovery for 48–72 hours. This is a data point, not a judgment.
Deload Rules
TriggerResponse
Scheduled Deload (Week 4)Reduce total volume by 40%. Keep intensity identical to the week prior. Do not add training to fill the "extra" time.
3 consecutive poor sleep nightsDrop the week's hardest session. Replace with easy 20 min run or full rest.
Readiness score below 5 for 3 daysUnplanned deload. One week at 50% volume. Re-assess.
Any pain that alters gait or movement patternStop. Rest 48 hours. If pain persists, seek professional assessment before continuing.

Section 07
The Upgrade Path

The Athletic Rebuild System will produce real results for athletes who follow it with discipline. But there is a ceiling to self-coached training — and knowing when you have hit it is its own skill. Here is how to know.

The Athletic Rebuild System vs. The Athletic Rebuild (1:1 Coaching)
What Changes When
a Coach Is In the Room
The Athletic Rebuild System (This Document)
  • Proven 12-week framework
  • Periodized phases with clear structure
  • Nutrition protocol and recovery system
  • Weekly self-check-in process
  • Self-assessment at four checkpoints
  • Re-usable for multiple training cycles
The Athletic Rebuild (1:1 Coaching)
  • Custom plan built around your exact baseline
  • Weekly adjustments based on your real data
  • Form video review and technique correction
  • Weekly check-in calls with Coach Tyler
  • Direct messaging access between sessions
  • Nutrition plan personalized to your body weight, goals, and schedule
  • ARA Community access
Signs You Have Outgrown Self-Guided Training
You are plateauingYou followed a full cycle and did not hit your goal. The issue is almost never effort — it is diagnosis. A coach sees what you cannot.
Recurring injuryThe same thing keeps breaking down. This is a movement or load management problem. Self-coaching this is difficult without an outside eye on your form.
High-stakes goalYou have a race with a specific time goal, or a personal milestone that actually matters to you. The margin for error is smaller. A coach narrows it.
AccountabilityYou know what to do and consistently do not do it. A check-in call with a coach changes the math on skipped sessions faster than any app.
You want to go fasterSpecifically faster. Not just finish. Not just PR slightly. You have a number and want to build the exact program that produces it.

I built this because I know what it is like to want to train seriously and not have access to a coach. I dropped 1:18 off my marathon using these principles before I became a coach. The framework works.
