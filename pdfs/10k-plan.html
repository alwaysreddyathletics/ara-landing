Who This Is For
  • Experience required: You've run a 5K before or have been training consistently for at least 4 weeks
  • Time commitment: 4 sessions per week, 45–60 minutes each
  • Equipment: Running shoes and GPS watch (recommended) or phone with running app
  • Progression: Base building → Tempo development → Interval work → Peak week → Taper
The Structure
Weeks 1–2
Base Building

Four easy runs per week with a long run on Friday. The goal is aerobic base, not performance. Easy pace throughout. Cross-training on Saturday is optional — walking, cycling, swimming.

Weeks 3–4
Tempo Development

Tempo runs introduced Wednesday. Week 4 is a deload — reduced volume to allow adaptation. Don't skip the deload because you feel good. It's there for a reason.

Weeks 5–6
Interval Work

Interval sessions replace Wednesday's tempo. 800m repeats at 5K pace. Long run increases to 5.5 miles. This is the hardest phase — the fitness you build here shows up on race day.

Week 7
Peak Week

Your highest-volume week. Longest tempo run (5 miles total) and peak long run at 7 miles. Do the work, then trust the taper.

Week 8
Taper + Race

Volume drops hard. Three short, easy runs leading to race day. Your legs are ready. Your job is to show up fresh and execute your pacing strategy.

The Rule
Start Slower. Finish Stronger.

In every race, in every tempo session. The athlete who goes out controlled always passes the athlete who went out hot.

Session Key

Easy Run

Conversational pace. Full sentences. You could hold this for hours. Most of your weekly miles are here.

Tempo Run

Comfortably hard. 3–4 words per breath. Hold race effort for the tempo segment, not the warm-up or cool-down.

Intervals (800m)

800 meters = half a mile. Run each repeat at your best current 5K race pace. Full 90 seconds rest between each.

A Note from Coach Tyler

PRs Don't Happen By Accident.

Eight weeks of deliberate, progressive work — built with a deload so your body adapts, an interval block so your legs find a gear they forgot they had, and a peak week that will feel hard until you hit race day and realize why you did it.

The athletes who nail the 10K are almost always the ones who respected the easy days. Run your easy runs easy so your hard runs can be hard. That's the whole game. Every session in this plan has a purpose. Don't improvise.

See you on the other side of week 8.
