8-Week 10K
Training Plan

Step It Up.

An 8-week plan built for runners who have a base and want to race a 10K properly. Four runs per week. Tempo work, interval sessions, and a long run that peaks at 7 miles before a full taper. This is the plan that teaches you how to actually race 6.2 miles.

Who This Is For
You've run a 5K. Now race a 10K.

This plan assumes you can run 3 miles comfortably before starting. If you've completed the 6-Week 5K plan or have been running consistently for at least a month, you're ready for this.

— Experience required
You've run a 5K or have been running 3–4 times per week for at least 4 weeks.

— Time commitment
4 runs per week, 45–60 minutes each. Long run peaks at 7 miles in Week 7.

— Pace guidance
Easy = conversational. Tempo = comfortably hard. Intervals = your best current 5K race effort.

— The only rule
Start slower than you think on race day. Athletes who go out controlled always pass athletes who went out hot.

The Structure
PRs Don't Happen by Accident.
Weeks 1–2
Base Building

Four easy runs per week. Long run builds from 3 to 4 miles. Aerobic base only — no intensity yet. Optional cross-training on Saturday.

Weeks 3–4
Tempo Development

Tempo sessions introduced Wednesday. Week 4 is a deload — reduced volume to let your body absorb the work. Don't skip the deload.

Weeks 5–6
Interval Work

800m interval sessions replace Wednesday tempo. Long run increases to 5.5 miles. This phase builds the gear you'll use to race.

Week 7
Peak Week

Highest volume week. Longest tempo run (5 miles total) and peak long run at 7 miles. Do the work, then trust the taper.

Week 8
Taper + Race Week

Volume drops hard. Three short easy runs. Your legs are ready — your job now is to show up fresh and execute the race.

Session Key
Session TypeWhat It MeansEffort Level
Easy RunConversational pace. Full sentences throughout. The majority of your weekly miles. Most athletes run these too fast — slow down.4–5 out of 10
Tempo RunThe structure is always: easy warm-up miles + tempo segment + easy cool-down miles. The tempo segment is comfortably hard — a few words, not sentences.7–8 out of 10
Intervals (800m)800 meters = half a mile. Run each at your best current 5K effort. Full 90 seconds rest between each repeat. Don't cut the rest short.8–9 out of 10
Cross-TrainOptional. Cycling, swimming, rowing, or walking. Easy effort only. Keeps you moving without adding running stress.3–5 out of 10
A Note from Coach Tyler
PRs don't happen by accident.

Eight weeks of deliberate, progressive work — a deload to let your body adapt, an interval block to find a gear you forgot you had, and a peak week that will feel hard until you hit race day and realize why you did it.

The athletes who nail the 10K are almost always the ones who respected the easy days. Run your easy runs easy so your hard runs can be hard. Every session in this plan has a purpose. Run it as written.

See you on the other side of week eight.
