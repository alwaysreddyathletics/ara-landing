The Only Goal
Who This Is For
Your first — or fastest — 5K. Done right.

This plan is built for athletes returning to running or stepping onto the race course for the first time. Three runs per week. Easy miles to build the base. One tempo session per week from week three onward. Everything is under 45 minutes.

— No base required
You should be able to run/walk 2 miles before starting. If you can do that, this plan will do the rest.

— Time commitment
3 runs per week, each under 45 minutes. Long run peaks at 4 miles in Week 5.

— Pace guidance
Easy = conversational. Tempo = comfortably hard — you can say a few words but not full sentences.

— The only goal
Cross the finish line strong. Everything else — pace, time, place — comes later.

The Structure
Simple. Progressive. Effective.
Weeks 1–2
Build the Base

Three easy runs per week. Low mileage, conversational pace. Your body adapts to consistent running — no intensity yet.

Weeks 3–4
Build Phase — Tempo Introduced

Wednesday run becomes a tempo session. Mileage increases slightly on Friday. This is where the fitness starts showing.

Week 5
Peak Week

Longest tempo run and your highest-mileage long run before the taper. Push through it — the race-day legs are built here.

Week 6
Taper + Race Week

Volume drops significantly. Two short easy runs, a shakeout, then race day. Trust the taper — the fitness is already in your legs.

Pace Guide
Run to Effort. Not to the Watch.

You don't need a specific pace. You need a specific effort. Use this guide to calibrate every run.

Effort TypeWhat It Feels LikeWhen to Use It
Easy PaceFull conversation without effort. You could hold this for hours. If you're out of breath, you're going too fast.All Tuesday and Saturday runs, warm-up and cool-down miles of every tempo session
Tempo PaceComfortably hard. A few words at a time — not sentences. You should feel like you could hold this for 20 minutes but not an hour.The tempo segment of Wednesday runs only (weeks 3–5)

A Note from Coach Tyler
Six weeks. One decision.

Most people never run a 5K because they never start. The few who start usually quit in week two because they went out too fast, got too sore, and decided it wasn't for them.

That's not going to be you — because you have a plan that tells you exactly what to do and at exactly what effort level. Follow it. Don't run harder on the easy days because you feel good. Don't skip the warm-up. Hit the taper even though it feels counterintuitive. The program works when you trust it.

Six weeks from now, you will cross a finish line. It's already decided — the decision was made the moment you started week one. Now go run.
