Who This Is For
  • Experience required: None — this is your starting point for running with real structure
  • Time commitment: 3 sessions per week, each under 45 minutes
  • Equipment: Running shoes and a GPS watch or phone with a running app
  • Progression: Easy pacing and habit-building in weeks 1–2, tempo work introduced in week 3
The Structure
Weeks 1–2
Base Building

Three easy runs per week. The goal is getting your legs and lungs working together again without any intensity. Conversational pace throughout. Mileage is low on purpose — we're building the habit first.

Weeks 3–4
Build Phase

Tempo runs introduced on your midweek session. Mileage increases slightly on your long day. This is where you start finding out what you're working with — don't blow up the tempo sessions.

Week 5
Peak Week

Your hardest week. Longest tempo run and your peak long run before the taper. This is where the race-day fitness is built. Push, but don't destroy yourself — you need to taper from here.

Week 6
Taper + Race Week

Volume drops significantly. The fitness is in your legs — your job now is to show up fresh. Two short easy runs plus a race day shakeout, then race day. Trust the taper.

Pace Guide

You don't need to run to a specific pace. You need to run to a specific effort level. Here's how to calibrate:

Easy Pace

You can hold a full conversation without effort. You could run this for hours. If you're out of breath, you're going too fast. Most first-time runners run their easy days 60–90 seconds per mile too fast — the goal is aerobic base, not performance.

Tempo Pace

Comfortably hard. You can say a few words but not full sentences. You should feel like you could hold this for 20–30 minutes but couldn't sustain it for an hour. This is the pace that builds race fitness. Don't sandbag the tempo sessions.

A Note from Coach Tyler

Six Weeks. One Decision.

Most people never run a 5K because they never start. The few who start usually quit in week 2 because they went out too fast, got too sore, and decided it wasn't for them. That's not going to be you — because you have a plan that tells you exactly what to do and at exactly what effort level.

Follow it. Don't run harder on the easy days because you feel good. Don't skip the warm-up because you're short on time. Hit the taper even though it feels counterintuitive. The program works when you trust the program.

Six weeks from now, you will cross a finish line. It's already decided — the decision was made the moment you downloaded this plan. Now go run.

— Coach Tyler Reddy · Always Reddy Athletics
