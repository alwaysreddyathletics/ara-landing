Section 1 · Race Week Schedule
The Week Before the Race

Race week isn't for getting fitter. It's for showing up fresh. The fitness is already in your legs — your job this week is to protect it, stay sharp, and not do anything stupid.

DaySessionKey Notes
MONEasy run 20–30 min + 4 strides (100m each)Last real training stimulus. Easy conversational pace. Strides should feel fast but not hard.
TUERest or 20 min light yoga/mobility onlyNo running. Let the Monday session absorb.
WEDEasy 20 min + 3 miles at race goal paceRemind your legs what race pace feels like. Not a workout — just a reminder.
THUComplete restOff. Eat a full, normal day. Sleep 8+ hours. Don't try to "make up" anything.
FRIShakeout run: 10–15 min easy + 4 stridesJust to move. No effort. The goal is loose legs, not fitness.
SATRACE DAY — See Race Morning Timeline belowExecute the plan. Trust the training.
SUNFull rest + optional 20 min recovery walkSee Post-Race Recovery section.

If your race is on Sunday: Shift the whole schedule forward one day. Keep Friday as your last easy run and Saturday as complete rest.

Section 2 · Race Morning Timeline
Hour by Hour to the Start Line

Times are relative to gun time. If your race starts at 8:00am, Gun = 8:00am and work backwards.

Gun − 3:00
Wake up. Eat your race-day breakfast — whatever you tested in training. Nothing new on race day. 16–20oz water immediately. Coffee if it's your routine.
Gun − 2:30
Finish eating. Hydrate steadily. Lay out all gear — bib, shoes, watch, nutrition, safety pins. Verify everything now, not at the venue.
Gun − 2:00
Arrive at venue. Locate start area, bag drop, port-a-potties, and your corral. First bathroom trip here — lines are shorter now than they will be later.
Gun − 1:30
Begin dynamic warm-up sequence (see Section 3). Takes 15–20 minutes. Do not skip this. Cold muscles into race pace is how injuries happen and races fall apart.
Gun − 1:00
Bag drop. Final gear check — bib pinned, watch GPS acquired, laces double-knotted. Small snack (gel, banana) if your race is 10K or longer.
Gun − 0:30
Last bathroom stop. Get in line. This is your final chance before the start — don't skip it.
Gun − 0:20
Easy jog 5–8 minutes at true conversational pace. Legs moving, breathing settling, mind focusing.
Gun − 0:10
Final 4 strides at race pace: 80–100m each, 60 seconds rest between. You should feel fast and ready — not tired. Move to your corral.
Gun − 0:05
Corral, settle, breathe. Review your pacing plan one final time. Trust the training. The work is done — this is the easy part.
GUN TIME
Start 15–20 seconds per mile slower than goal pace. You will thank yourself at the halfway point.
Section 3 · Warm-Up Protocol
Pre-Race Activation Sequence

Complete in order. Total time: 15–20 minutes. The goal: raise core temperature, activate hips and glutes, prime the nervous system for race pace effort.

Easy jog5–8 minutesCardiovascular warm-up, body temperature
Race pace strides4 × 80–100mNeuromuscular priming, race pace feel

Short on time? Minimum effective warm-up: leg swings (all directions), high knees, butt kicks, 5 min easy jog, 4 strides. Under any circumstances, never go straight to race pace from a standing start.

Section 4 · Pacing Strategy
How to Actually Race It

The single most common race day mistake: going out too fast in mile 1. The adrenaline, the crowd energy, the fresh legs — everything tells you to push. The athletes who listen blow up by mile 4. Don't be that athlete.

Miles 1–2
Run 15–20 sec/mile SLOWER than goal pace
This feels wrong. That's correct. You're banking energy. Your cardiovascular system needs 8–12 minutes to fully engage — running at goal pace before that means you're already in oxygen debt before you've gone anywhere.
Middle Miles
Lock into goal pace — hold it there
You'll feel good. The urge is to push. Resist it. Check your watch every mile, not every 400m. Discipline in the middle miles is what builds a PR — not heroics.
Final Mile
Empty the tank — only now
If you executed correctly, you have something left. Use it. Run the last mile as hard as you can sustain to the line. If you have nothing — you raced correctly. Cross the line with everything you had.
Section 5 · Post-Race Recovery
Protect the Work You've Done

First 30 Minutes

  • Keep walking 10–15 min minimum
  • Quick carbs within 20 min
  • Protein within 30 min
  • Water + electrolytes
  • Change out of wet clothes
  • Celebrate — you earned it

Hours 2–24

  • Real meal within 2 hours
  • Compression socks if available
  • Ice bath 10–15 min (optional)
  • Light stretch before bed
  • 8–9 hours sleep
  • No intensity today

Days 2–7

  • Day 2: Rest or 15 min walk
  • Day 3: Light yoga/movement
  • Day 4–5: Easy 20 min jog
  • Day 6–7: Normal easy pace
  • No intensity until Day 7
  • Plan your next race
