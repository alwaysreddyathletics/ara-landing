Section 1 · Race Week Schedule
The Week Before the Race.

Race week isn't for getting fitter. The fitness is already in your legs. Your only job this week is to protect it, stay sharp, and show up fresh.

Section 2 · Race Morning Timeline
Hour by Hour to the Start Line.

All times are relative to your gun time (G). If your race starts at 8:00am, G = 8:00am. Work backwards from there.

G − 3:00Wake up. Eat your tested race-day breakfast — nothing new today. 16–20oz water immediately. Coffee if it's your routine.
G − 2:30Finish eating. Hydrate steadily. Lay out all gear — bib, shoes, watch, nutrition, safety pins. Verify everything now.
G − 2:00Arrive at venue. Locate start, bag drop, port-a-potties, your corral. First bathroom trip here — lines are shorter now.
G − 1:30Begin dynamic warm-up sequence (Section 3). This takes 15–20 minutes. Do not skip it. Cold muscles into race pace is how races fall apart.
G − 1:00Bag drop. Final gear check — bib pinned, GPS acquired, laces double-knotted. Small snack if your race is 10K or longer.
G − 0:30Last bathroom stop. Get in line. This is your final chance.
G − 0:20Easy jog 5–8 minutes at conversational pace. Legs moving, breathing settling, mind focusing.
G − 0:10Final 4 strides at race pace: 80–100m each, 60 seconds rest between. You should feel fast and ready — not tired. Move to corral.
G − 0:05Corral, settle, breathe. Review pacing plan one final time. Trust the training. The work is done.
GUN TIMEStart 15–20 seconds per mile slower than goal pace. You will thank yourself at the halfway mark.

Section 4 · Pacing Strategy
How to Actually Race It.

The single most common race day mistake: going out too fast in mile one. The adrenaline, the crowd energy, the fresh legs — everything tells you to push. The athletes who listen blow up before the halfway mark.

Miles 1–2 (or first 20%)
Run 15–20 seconds per mile SLOWER than goal pace

This feels wrong. That's correct. You're banking energy. Your cardiovascular system needs 8–12 minutes to fully engage — running at goal pace before that means you're already in oxygen debt before you've gone anywhere. The athletes who go out controlled always pass the athletes who went out hot.

Middle Miles
Lock into goal pace and hold it there

You'll feel good in the middle. The urge is to push. Resist it. Check your watch every mile, not every 400 meters. Discipline in the middle miles is what builds a PR — not heroics at mile two.

Final Mile
Empty the tank — but only now

If you executed correctly, you'll have something left. Use it. Run the last mile as hard as you can sustain to the finish line. Cross with everything you had.

Section 5 · Post-Race Recovery
Protect the Work You've Done.

How you recover determines how fast you get back to training. Most athletes treat the finish line as the endpoint. The work you do in the next 7 days sets you up for everything that comes next.

First 30 Minutes

  • Keep walking 10–15 min minimum
  • Quick carbs within 20 min
  • Protein within 30 min
  • Water + electrolytes
  • Change out of wet clothes
  • Celebrate — you earned it

Hours 2–24

  • Real meal within 2 hours
  • Compression socks if available
  • Ice bath 10–15 min (optional)
  • Light stretch before bed
  • 8–9 hours sleep
  • No intensity today

Days 2–7

  • Day 2: Rest or 15 min walk only
  • Day 3: Light yoga / mobility
  • Day 4–5: Easy jog 20 min if ready
  • Day 6–7: Return to normal easy
  • No intensity until Day 7
  • Plan your next race
A Note from Coach Tyler
Race day isn't the test. It's the reward.

You did the training. You put in the early mornings, the tempo sessions that made your lungs burn, the long runs that ate into your weekends. Race day is just the day you get to show what you've built. The work was already done before you crossed the start line.

Use this guide. Trust your preparation. Go find out what you're made of — and when you cross the finish line, already be thinking about what's next.
